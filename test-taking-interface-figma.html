A study of the consumption of smartphones in five countries found that in each country, weights of discarded smartphones decreased significantly over a ten-year period. The researchers concluded that manufacturers had been making smartphones lighter. However, a critic of the study points out that the weights recorded were the weights of smartphones at the time they were discarded, not at the time they were purchased. The critic argues that the decrease in weight could be explained by the fact that newer smartphones are kept for shorter periods before being discarded. Batteries and other components degrade over time, and a smartphone that has been used for three years will weigh slightly less than it did when new due to battery swelling, case wear, and loss of small components. The critic's argument requires which of the following assumptions?

Which one of the following is an assumption required by the critic's argument?
The decrease in weight of discarded smartphones was not primarily caused by manufacturers using lighter materials in newer models.
Consumers in all five countries replaced their smartphones at approximately the same rate during the ten-year period.
The weight loss experienced by a smartphone over three years of use is large enough to account for the observed decrease in discarded smartphone weights.
Manufacturers did not simultaneously make smartphones lighter while consumers also began discarding them sooner.
None of the five countries in the study experienced significant changes in the demographics of smartphone consumers during the ten-year period.
